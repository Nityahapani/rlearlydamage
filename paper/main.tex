\begin{abstract}
A temporarily impaired reinforcement-learning agent can lag a clean control because of foregone progress, additional recovery cost, or both. We distinguish same-update gap attribution from interventions on detected harm relative to a historical pause benchmark. Our primary evidence is one preregistered Asterix PPO study with 32 new training seeds, reward-sign corruption and a fixed 200-update recovery horizon. Native pause-relative recovery cost is $-1.72\ci{-1.94}{-1.50}$ return points. Restoring only the pre-impairment actor, without the clean fork's additional progress, improves recovery by $+1.58\ci{+1.37}{+1.79}$. A single four-component practical-effect criterion, requiring native and late harm, rescue and reciprocal transfer, passes at $p=3.32\times10^{-8}$, $\alpha=.01$. The intervention removes 91.8\% of the measured mean cost (paired-bootstrap 95\% interval 86.3--97.5\%), not 91.8\% of naturally stored damage. The interval jointly resamples paired training seeds conditional on a fixed evaluation batch. A small residual remains; matched-state practical equivalence fails. Specified optimizer, world-reset and second-evaluation checks retain positive rescue. A single donor/reference diagram and an integrated reproducibility appendix expose the counterfactual, uncertainty and artifact assumptions. Earlier studies retain their heterogeneous evidence status; there is no paper-wide multiplicity control. The conclusion is a setting- and intervention-specific repair result, not a general RL recovery law, proof of irreversible damage, a new-task plasticity result or a unique internal localization of impairment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A clean agent continues learning while an impaired copy falls behind. Replacing the impaired copy's parameters with the clean copy's later parameters may close this gap simply by importing the progress it missed. Such a transplant can identify a useful intervention without identifying a cause of recovery cost \emph{beyond} lost training time. A reference trajectory is therefore part of the scientific claim, not a cosmetic plotting choice.

We ask a narrower question: \emph{in one selected setting with detected pause-relative harm, does the learner's own pre-impairment actor checkpoint repair its subsequent recovery cost?} In Asterix PPO, a fixed 32-seed confirmation supports this intervention. The actor donor predates reward corruption; all other primary removal-time fields come from the naturally impaired learner. The primary requires four effects together, rather than selecting the most favourable endpoint. It is met, with native cost negative and actor rescue positive in all 32 seeds. The result is substantial but not complete restoration, and it does not locate a unique internal representation of impairment.

The main text presents the reference framework, this one study, its scope and the evidence hierarchy. The accompanying \artifact{paper/supplement.pdf}{Supplementary Material} preserves the full historical protocols, all result tables, negative/inconclusive primaries, context checks, registration timelines and deviations. Earlier experiments motivate the selected case; they are not pooled into its primary or treated as equally strong independent confirmations.

\paragraph{Contributions and scope.}
We provide an explicit reference-aware intervention framework; a fixed 32-seed confirmation that an older actor can repair measured recovery cost without extra clean-run progress; and an auditable path from paired curves to statistical decisions and state-integrity checks. The contribution is neither the invention of parameter resetting nor a general algorithm guaranteed to preserve plasticity. Section~\ref{m:contribution} distinguishes our causal target from fresh resets, whole-checkpoint rollback and new-task plasticity studies. The present evidence remains one prior-selected Asterix PPO condition.

\subsection{What the reported AUC measures}\label{m:auc}
Let $w_0$ be impairment onset, $w_1=w_0+L$ removal, and $V_i^X(k)$ the mean unrestricted evaluation return of seed $i$, arm $X$, at recovery checkpoint $k$. In the primary study,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal K=\{10,20,\ldots,200\},\qquad
A_i(X)=\frac{1}{20}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}V_i^X(k).
\end{equation}
Our ``recovery AUC'' is this \textbf{discrete time-average}, not an unnormalised integral or a trapezoidal area. It retains units of \textbf{evaluation episode-return points}. We average paired seed differences, not episodes or checkpoints as independent observations. The removal evaluation $k=0$ is plotted but excluded from AUC. The late endpoint averages the last five checkpoints, $k=160,170,180,190,200$.

The native cost and same-update gap use different benchmarks:
\begin{align}
E_i^{\rm native}&=A_i(N)-A_i(H), & H_i(k)&=C_i(w_0+k),\\
E_i^{\rm same}&=A_i(N)-A_i(C_{\rm same}), & C_{{\rm same},i}(k)&=C_i(w_1+k).
\end{align}
A negative $E^{\rm native}$ is extra cost relative to this historical lost-time benchmark. It is not a guarantee that a literal paused runner in every future world would follow $H$. The matched onset-state reference is measured separately for precisely this reason.

\paragraph{Scale and practical meaning.}
An Asterix effect of $-1.72$ means that average evaluation return is 1.72 points below the specified reference across those 20 checkpoints. It is \emph{not} 1.72 training steps, the total training reward lost, or a time-to-recovery estimate. One PPO update is 8{,}192 environment steps, so the 200-update recovery spans 1{,}638{,}400 steps. The registered .50 AUC margin was calibrated against a prior clean score of about 6.0 (roughly 8\%); the .30 late margin was 5\% of that same prior scale. These calibrations are specific to this game. Raw AUC magnitudes from SpaceInvaders, CartPole or PQN studies cannot be ranked or pooled as if they shared reward scale, evaluation policy and recovery grid. Cross-algorithm recovery comparisons additionally require matching \emph{environment steps}, not update counts.

\subsection{Intervention effects are not storage fractions}
A clean-fork swap, a past-actor swap, a factorial main effect and a Shapley allocation answer different questions. The primary study uses a \emph{background-specific} actor swap in the natural impaired context; it is not a full three-component allocation. Its repaired fraction is
\begin{equation}
\rho=\frac{\overline{A(R)-A(N)}}{-\overline{E^{\rm native}}},
\end{equation}
where $R$ denotes actor restoration. It is a ratio of mean intervention effects, not the average of seedwise percentages, a Shapley value, or the proportion of impairment encoded in actor weights. Changed future experience is part of the treatment effect, not held fixed by definition.

\section{The preregistered Asterix study}\label{m:design}
\paragraph{Fixed selected condition.}
PPO \citep{schulman2017ppo} uses separate $2\times128$ ReLU actor and critic MLPs, Adam at constant learning rate $5\times10^{-4}$, 64 environments $\times$ 128 steps, four epochs and eight minibatches. The environment is Asterix-MinAtar in gymnax \citep{young2019minatar,lange2022gymnax}, implemented in JAX in the PureJaxRL style \citep{lu2022discovered}. Training rewards have their signs flipped during updates $[390,440)$ (409{,}600 steps), followed by 200 unrestricted updates. Evaluation always uses true reward, clean observations and the full action set. Every ten updates, 64 stochastic-policy evaluation episodes use key 10000. The analysis pairs independent training seeds conditional on this fixed evaluation batch.

All 32 unused IDs 1200--1231 and all 11 requested cells were registered before target data. A previous independent eight-seed Asterix cohort motivated this selected condition; it is not pooled into the primary and does not make this a new-task generalization test. No low-competence seed was removed, no primary analysis occurred before all 32 completed, and no setting, endpoint, sample-size or evaluation-key switch was used.

\paragraph{Donors and natural continuation.}
$P$ is the shared onset state at update 390, $D$ the impaired state at 440, and $C^+$ the unimpaired fork at 440. The main repair replaces \emph{only actor parameters in $D$ with those from $P$}; it does not use the extra-progress actor from $C$. The removal-time critic, Adam state, environment, observations, future RNG and counters remain those of $D$. The all-impaired cell is asserted equal over the complete runner-state tree to untouched natural $D$. Actor/critic/optimizer composition is explicit in code; after intervention these fields and collected experience can diverge normally.

For reciprocal transfer, the impaired actor is inserted into a learner with onset critic parameters and Adam state, under the same natural $D$ world/RNG/counter context. We denote that matched learner reference by $Q_D$: it is \emph{not} the full onset runner $P$ or the historical trajectory $H$. In archived pause-cell codes, C means an onset donor and D an impaired donor: DDD is native, CDD actor-restored, CCC the matched reference, and DCC reciprocal impaired-actor transfer.

\paragraph{One primary claim, four required parts.}
The registration fixes four one-sided paired-$t$ tests against practical-effect thresholds (Table~\ref{m:primary}). The single intersection--union claim uses $p_{\rm claim}=\max_jp_j$ at $\alpha=.01$, with an additional requirement of at least 24/32 native-negative and 24/32 rescue-positive seeds. All parts must pass. Intervals are nominal training-seed intervals, with 99\% intervals, exact signs, paired bootstrap and simultaneous-bound sensitivities retained in the supplement and analysis artifact \citep{henderson2018matters,agarwal2021precipice}. The fixed second evaluation key 54321, common-fresh-Adam factorial and reset-world rescue are prespecified \emph{secondary} checks; they cannot replace a failed primary.

\paragraph{Robustness does not imply neutrality or full restoration.}
All secondary results remain visible in Table~\ref{m:secondary}. Fresh Adam, a reset world and the second evaluation key retain positive rescue intervals. The smaller negative fresh-Adam critic effect is not declared absent. Neither a nonsignificant context difference nor positive rescue in another context establishes context neutrality. The second key is not selected or pooled into the primary.

The repaired-minus-historical residual is small but negative. Its secondary $\pm.30$ TOST margin passes, while the same margin against $Q_D$ \emph{fails} \citep{lakens2017equivalence}. Practical-margin agreement is not literal equality. In particular, positive rescue and a 91.8\% reduction do not establish complete recovery, even though the primary compound passes.

\section{Evidence hierarchy across the research record}\label{m:evidence}
The primary result above is the strongest evidence for the \emph{named intervention}. The broader record is heterogeneous in sample size, design status, reference and inferential target. Table~\ref{m:hierarchy} prevents a favourable result in one tier from upgrading an unresolved result in another. A preregistered design is not automatically a successful outcome. New contrasts on reused histories are not independent-seed replications, and selected eligible conditions are not a task-prevalence sample.

\paragraph{No paper-wide error-control claim.}
The 32-seed compound is one separate registered $\alpha=.01$ claim. Only the five Phase-13 primaries share their specified Holm family \citep{holm1979simple}; older studies have other local families, exploratory analyses and disclosed interim looks. We neither combine these into a retrospective paper-wide false-positive guarantee nor count favourable panels as exchangeable independent confirmations. The main SpaceInvaders movement result, for example, has historical-pause AUC $+1.9\ci{-.7}{+4.4}$, $n=12$: harm worse than $-3$ is excluded, but equivalence is not established. Its 90\% actor allocation concerns a Tier-I gap, not demonstrated beyond-time harm.

\paragraph{Reanalysis is not rollout replay.}
All original primary seed-level means, reference alignment data, statistical source and recorded integrity checks are available. The original experiment did not retain full serialized donor trees. Appendix~\ref{app:availability} distinguishes that fact from later, checked numerical-state reconstructions and gives their actual completion/publication status. A fingerprint is not a checkpoint download. Loading a validated numerical donor can avoid retraining its prehistory, but regenerating its recovery trajectory or evaluating another random tape still requires computation. The self-contained appendix supplies the cell map, state fields, joint bootstrap, complete paired seed table and restoration commands; the separate historical supplement is not needed for primary statistical reanalysis.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}\label{m:conclusion}
\paragraph{What the result establishes.}
Under one declared natural future context, a learner's own pre-impairment actor checkpoint causes a substantial improvement in measured pause-relative recovery. Reciprocal transfer and the specified checks constrain a simple extra-clean-progress or optimizer/world-only explanation. The repair removes most, not all, of this mean cost. It remains a total downstream effect of this field-level intervention; it is not a causal accounting of uniquely stored impairment information.

\paragraph{What it does not establish.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Generality:} the strongest confirmation is one Asterix environment implementation, PPO, deterministic reward-sign corruption, small MLPs and one 200-update recovery horizon. It is not a law for RL agents, other architectures, impairments or training budgets.
\item \textbf{Context independence:} historical pause and matched learner references are different; natural, standardized and reset futures answer different interventions. Positive checks do not establish a context-neutral decomposition.
\item \textbf{Irreversibility or new-task plasticity:} an AUC cost and a late-window deficit do not prove permanent damage. No experiment measures preserved capacity to learn a new task after impairment.
\item \textbf{Mechanism or storage:} actor replacement changes future data and learning. Replay time courses, inconclusive label-exposure primaries and policy-KL associations do not identify a unique internal representation or natural storage location of impairment.
\item \textbf{Collective certainty or deployment:} heterogeneous local test families provide no paper-wide error guarantee or task-prevalence estimate. The fixed evaluation pools and unavailable full donor trees limit reproduction; no automatic checkpoint-selection or deployment rollback policy is validated.
\end{itemize}

The defensible conclusion is specific: \emph{a past-actor intervention repairs 91.8\% of the measured mean native recovery cost in this registered Asterix PPO experiment}, with a small residual and failed matched-state practical equivalence explicitly retained. The interval is conditional on the fixed evaluation batch and jointly resamples numerator/denominator seed rows; changing the reference changes the repair fraction. The findings do \textbf{not} establish irreversible damage, preserved plasticity on new tasks, or a unique internal representation of impairment. The framework is useful because it makes these boundaries explicit, not because it turns one successful restoration into a general recovery law.

\subsubsection*{Broader impact statement}
These are controlled small-agent experiments in simulated environments. Potential use in reliable RL recovery requires evaluation of checkpoint selection, new tasks, longer horizons and deployment risks; no safety guarantee follows from this study.
